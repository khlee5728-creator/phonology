§7.6 Stress in Compounds — (1/2) Noun & Adjective Compounds

Compound = more than one root morpheme (usually two free morphemes), but functions like a single word. (Yavaš p. 191)

Three writing conventions:
  • one word — blueprint
  • with hyphen — fail-safe
  • with space — flower girl
Stress ↔ writing tendency:
  • 1st-element stress → joined / hyphenated
  • 2nd-element stress → written with space
Noun compounds (90% of all compounds)

All receive stress on the first element:

N + Nphónecard, mátchbox, téapot, póstman
Adj + Nwhíte house
V + Nstóp watch
Part/Adv + Nóverdose, únderwear

Exceptions (mostly names): Lake Érie, Mount Sínai, Great Brítain.

Adjective compounds

Stress on the first element:

N + Adjnátionwide, séasick, bédridden
Adj + Adjréd hot
Prep + Adjóverripe
교재 §7.6 ¶1-3 (p. 191). Compound 정의 + 표기 convention. Noun compounds가 90%로 압도적. 모두 첫 요소 강세. 예외는 거의 항상 names. Adjective compounds도 동일 (첫 요소).

§7.6 Stress in Compounds — (2/2) Verb / 3+ Element / Complex

Verb compounds — most first-element, one exception
Combination Stress Examples

Almost always stressed on the first element:

Stress on the first element of the original noun compound:

(a) N+N"asséembly line worker"
(b) Adj+N"hígh school student"
(c) V+N"píck up truck"

Nestable: hígh school student essay, asséembly line worker dispute

교재 §7.6 ¶4-6 (pp. 191-192). Verb compounds: N+V, Adj+V, V+V는 첫 요소 강세 (다른 그룹과 동일). 단, Part/Adv+V는 둘째 요소 강세 (undertake, oversleep) — 핵심 예외. 3+ element compounds: 거의 항상 첫 요소 (fly-by-night, mother-in-law 등). Complex compounds: 두 noun compound가 결합하여 형성, 첫 요소 강세 유지. Nested 가능 (high school student essay).

§7.7 AmE vs. BrE Stress Differences — (1/3) Groups (a) (b) (c)

Several English words receive different stresses in AmE and BrE — patterns fall into 5 groups. (Yavaš pp. 192–195)

Group Pattern Examples
(a) 2-syll N (French origin)
AmE final / BE penult		 brochúre, ballét, café, chatéau, cliché, cornét, crochét, debrís, fillét, frontíer, garáge, plateáu, premíer, salón, soufflé, toupée, vermóuth, valét
(b) Reverse: BE final / AmE initial wéekend, pótluck, príncess, récess, résearch, áddress, ínquiry, móustache; (longer) ártisan, Pórtuguese, cígarette, mágazine, cóntroversy
(c) 2-syll V ending in -ate
AmE initial / BE final		 cástrate, crémate, díctate, dónate, frústrate, gýrate, mígrate, púlsate, rótate, tránslate, vácate, víbrate, lócate, mútate
Exceptions (both final): confláte, creáte, debáte, defláte, eláte, reláte, abáte, infláte, negáte
교재 §7.7 (a)-(c) (pp. 192-193). Group (a): 2음절 N French origin — AmE는 final, BE는 penult. brochure, ballet 등. Group (b): 정반대 — BE final, AmE initial. weekend, princess 등. Group (c): 2음절 -ate V — AmE initial, BE final. castrate, dictate 등. 예외 (conflate 등은 둘 다 final).

§7.7 AmE vs. BrE Stress Differences — (2/3) Group (d): -ary / -ery / -ory / -mony

Group (d): Forms ending in -ary / -ery / -ory / -mony receive a secondary stress on the penult in AmE, but the same syllable has a reduced vowel in BE. (Yavaš pp. 193–194)

Example: secondary [sékəndɛ̀ɹi] (AmE) vs [sékəndəɹi] (BE) → [sékəndɹi] (3 syll)

-arynécessàry, árbitràry, díctionàry, Fébruàry, líterary, mílitàry, órdinàry, prímàry, sécretàry, témporàry, mómentàry, légendàry, sédentàry, vísionàry, contémporàry
-erycémetèry, conféctionèry, dýsentèry, mónastèry, státionèry
-oryállegòry, cátegòry, dórmitòry, mándatòry, obsérvatòry, térritòry, prómissòry, compénsatòry, explóratòry, consérvatòry
-monyácrimòny, álimòny, céremòny, mátrimòny, téstimòny
Exceptions (no secondary stress in AmE either — primary stress directly before the suffix): salary, bravery, ivory, lottery, elementary, anniversary, documentary, complimentary, infirmary
교재 §7.7 (d) (pp. 193-194). -ary/-ery/-ory/-mony — AmE는 secondary stress, BE는 reduced vowel. secondary [sékəndɛ̀ɹi] (AmE) vs [sékəndəɹi] (BE). 예외: primary stress가 suffix 바로 앞이면 secondary 없음 (salary, bravery, lottery).

§7.7 AmE vs. BrE Stress Differences — (3/3) Group (e): -ile + Other Varieties

Group (e) — -ile: REVERSE pattern

AmE has a reduced vowel, BE has secondary stress (opposite of (d)).

Example: agile [ǽʤəl] (AmE) / [ǽʤàɪl] (BE)

ágile, dócile, fértile, frágile, fútile, hóstile, móbile, míssile, stérile, vérsatile, vírile

Exception ([aɪ] in BOTH varieties): prófile, réptile, sénile

Other varieties (beyond AmE / BE)
  • Indian / Hong Kong / Singapore E.: -ate, -ize stressed on final syllable — alternáte, educáte, homogeníze, terroríze
  • Indian / Singapore E.: non-reduced vowels + non-initial stress on multisyllabic words (economic, faculty, necessary)
  • Fewer vowel reductions in Hawaii, Hong Kong, Singapore, India, Caribbean, African varieties → "syllable-timed" rhythm
교재 §7.7 (e) + 후반 (pp. 194-195). Group (e): -ile — REVERSE pattern. AmE reduced, BE secondary. agile [ǽʤəl] vs [ǽʤàɪl]. 예외 [aɪ] (둘 다 secondary): profile, reptile, senile. Other varieties: Indian/HK/Singapore — -ate, -ize final stress. Vowel reduction 적음 → syllable-timed rhythm.

§7.8 Intonation — (1/6) Tone Group & Tonic Accent

Intonation = pitch variations that occur over a phrase or sentence. (Yavaš p. 195, cf. Ch. 1)

Tone group & tonic syllable
  • Tone group (= intonational phrase) — a portion of a sentence over which an intonation contour extends
  • Within a tone group, each stressed syllable has a minor pitch increase, but one syllable has a major pitch change
  • The syllable with major pitch change = tonic syllable / tonic accent
Example (1) + default pattern

´ = stressed; * = tonic accent (major pitch change). boy, book are stressed; only teacher carries the tonic.

Default pattern: utterance-initial = shared (old) information; utterance-final = new information. → Tonic accent on the last stressed lexical item (N, V, Adj, Adv).

교재 §7.8 ¶1-3 (pp. 195-196). Intonation 정의 (Ch.1 재인용). Tone group = pitch contour 적용 단위. Tonic syllable = major pitch change. 예시 (1) "The boy gave the book to his *teacher" — boy, book은 minor stress, teacher가 tonic. Default: tonic은 utterance-final 마지막 stressed lexical item (= new info). 다음 슬라이드에서 이 default 변경 케이스.

§7.8 Intonation — (2/6) Shifting the Tonic Accent

The tonic accent can shift forward from its default position. Not all shifts involve contrast — tone groups are units of information, not syntax. (Yavaš p. 196)

Emphasis / contrast
Information-unit shift (not always contrast)

(3) I have *a party to plan / *letters to write.

(4a) Spinach is a *vegetable. (neutral)

(4b) *Spinach is a vegetable. (in discussion of vegetables)

Two exceptions to "last lexical item" default

(i) Intransitive V or VP with non-human subject:

(5a) Our *town is on an upswing.    (5b) The *bird flew away. (cf. the man *swore)

(ii) Sentential adverbials & adverbials of time in final position:

(6a) I don't watch *TV typically.    (6b) It wasn't a very nice *day unfortunately.

교재 §7.8 ¶4-7 (p. 196). Tonic accent의 default(마지막 lexical item)에서 앞으로 shift 가능. (1) Emphasis/contrast — 2b "somewhat" 강조. (2) Information unit 변경 — 3, 4a/4b. spinach 예시: vegetable이 카테고리 명사면 neutral은 vegetable에 tonic, but vegetable 종류 논의 시 spinach에 tonic. 두 예외: (i) intransitive V + non-human subject — town, bird; (ii) sentential/time adverbials final — TV/typically, day/unfortunately.

Falling contour = expresses finality / completion. Two types: full (long) fall vs short (low) fall. (Yavaš pp. 196-197)

Full (long) fall ↘↘

Declarative finality:

Emotional involvement:

wh-questions:

(9) Which way did she go?

Short (low) fall ↘

→ Long fall = involved/definitive; short fall = detached/neutral.

교재 §7.8 ¶8-10 (pp. 196-197). Falling intonation = finality. Full (long) fall = declarative finality + emotional involvement + wh-questions. Short (low) fall = detached, neutral mood. (10) "Whatever you say" — short fall로 "I agree with it" 의미.

Rising contour = non-definiteness, lack of assurance, incompletion. Addressee-oriented; degree of rise matches degree of uncertainty. (Yavaš pp. 197-198)

교재 §7.8 ¶11-14 (pp. 197-198). Rising = non-definiteness. High/long rise = puzzlement (11a/b "Is this a joke?"). Low/short rise = 더 common, 다양한 컨텍스트 — yes-no Q, echo Q, repetition Q, open-choice alternative (cf. closed choice = falling), tag Q (uncertainty), list items prior to last, readiness for new info.

§7.8 Intonation — (5/6) Combined Patterns & Level

Beyond pure falling and rising, English uses three more patterns: fall-rise, rise-fall, and level. (Yavaš pp. 198–199)

Fall–rise ↘↗

Agreement with reservation / hesitation — accepts but with caveat:

Rise–fall ↗↘

Dramatic equivalent of simple fall — strong approval / disapproval:

Level →

Bored or sarcastic attitude:

(21) A: John will be at the party.   B: Great.

교재 §7.8 ¶15-16 (pp. 198-199). 세 가지 combined/level patterns: (1) Fall-rise — agreement with reservation (19 "You can do it that way" = "but I don't think you should"). (2) Rise-fall — dramatic simple fall, 강한 approval/disapproval (20a/20b "That's wonderful" / "You can't do that"). (3) Level — bored/sarcastic (21 "Great"). 다음 슬라이드에서 "All right" 7가지 reading 종합.

§7.8 Intonation — (6/6) "All right": 7-Pattern Summary

Putting all intonation patterns together — the same words "All right" convey seven different meanings depending on contour. (Yavaš p. 199)

교재 §7.8 ¶17 (p. 199). "All right" 7가지 reading 종합: short fall (factual agreement) / long fall (enthusiastic) / sharp rise (rejection) / low rise (continuing/check) / fall-rise (doubtful) / rise-fall (emphatic enthusiasm) / level (uninspiring). 강세는 같은 말이지만 intonation으로 7가지 다른 의미 전달 — intonation의 표현력 종합 예시.

§7.9 Variations among the Varieties — (1/2) Specific Varieties & AmE vs BE

Significant intonation differences exist among varieties of English. (Yavaš pp. 199-200)

Specific varieties
  • Hawaii: yes-no Q typical terminal high rise → earlier rise + fall ("Would you like some tea?")
  • Welsh E.: tag Q with rise-fall (more emphatic)
  • Southern AmE: rising pitches frequency — "We played gámes and went for híkes and had the most wónderful tíme." (Bolinger 1998)
    → Asking for reassurance ("Are you paying attention?"). Extended to routines ("From Texas"). Australian E. shows the same.
AmE vs BE — main difference

BE: less use of high initial pitches, more use of final ones in yes-no questions.

"Would you like some coffee?"

AmE: ↗ (rising)

BE: different contour / no final rise

Cross-cultural misimpressions: AmE rising → BE sees as "too businesslike"; BE → AmE sees as "over-cordial", "unduly concerned" (Bolinger 1998).

교재 §7.9 ¶1-4 (pp. 199-200). 다양한 variety의 intonation 차이. - Hawaii: yes-no Q terminal high rise → earlier rise + fall. - Welsh E.: tag Q rise-fall (emphatic). - Southern AmE: rising pitches 빈도 ↑ (Bolinger 1998). reassurance 요청. Routine utterances로 확장 ("From Texas"). Australian E.도 동일. - AmE vs BE: BE는 high initial pitches 덜 사용, final ones (yes-no Q) 더. Cross-cultural misimpressions — AmE rising "too businesslike", BE "over-cordial". 다음 슬라이드: Bailey 1983 4-variety 종합 비교.

§7.9 Variations among the Varieties — (2/2) Bailey (1983) Four-Variety Contrast

Bailey (1983) shows how the same sentence spoken in four English varieties creates four different impressions on outsiders. (Yavaš p. 200)

"Is your mother feeling all right?" — four varieties
Outsider impressions

Variety별 intonation 차이. - Hawaii: yes-no Q terminal high rise → earlier rise + fall. - Welsh E.: tag Q rise-fall (emphatic). - Southern AmE: rising pitches 빈도 ↑ (Bolinger 1998). reassurance 요청. Routine utterances로 확장 ("From Texas"). Australian E.도 동일. - AmE vs BE: BE는 high initial pitches 덜 사용, final ones (yes-no Q) 더. Cross-cultural misimpressions — AmE rising "too businesslike", BE "over-cordial". - Bailey 1983: 4 varieties (British/Irish/Northern US/Southern US)의 같은 sentence 다른 contour → outsider impressions: condescending/incredulous/repetitive/surprised.
