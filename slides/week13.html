§7.1 Introduction — What is Stress?

Stress is a cover term for the prosodic features of duration, intensity, and pitch; the prominence of stressed syllables is generally manifested by their being longer, louder, and higher in pitch than unstressed syllables. (Yavaš p. 179)

Notation — Yavaš style (diacritics over vowels)
Symbol Meaning Example (Yavaš p. 179)
é  (acute) Primary stress democrat [démək.ɹ̣æt]
è  (grave) Secondary stress democratic [dèmək.ɹ̣ǽtɪk]
(no mark) Unstressed photograph [fótəgɹ̣æf]
[ə] Always unstressed (weightless) gratification [ɡɹ̣æ̀təfəkéʃən]

Dictionary style (ˈ before primary, ˌ before secondary) is not used in this course.

교재 §7.1 ¶1 (p. 179): Stress = duration·intensity·pitch의 cover term. 강세 음절은 unstressed보다 longer/louder/higher in pitch. Notation은 교재 §7.1 본문에는 별도 설명 없으나 책 전체의 표기 convention. 강의자료는 교재 스타일(acute/grave) 일관 사용. IPA 사전 스타일(ˈ, ˌ)은 사용하지 않음. [ə]가 weightless하다는 점은 §7.1 ¶8(Stressable syllable) 및 §7.2 Penult Rule에서 핵심으로 다시 등장. (참고 — 교재 ¶1 후반에 Speaker's POV: effort expended / Hearer's POV: perceptual prominence 두 관점 언급되지만, 후속 단원에서 다시 사용되지 않아 본 슬라이드에서는 제외.)

§7.1 Introduction — Variable & Mobile Stress

English has variable stress (any syllable in a polysyllabic word can carry stress) and mobile stress (morphologically related words shift stress + accompanying vowel reduction). (Yavaš p. 179)

Variable stress — trisyllabic nouns

Stress can fall on 1st, 2nd, or 3rd syllable:

article[áɹtɪkḷ]1st
tomato[təméɹo]2nd
kangaroo[kæ̀ŋgəɹ̣ú]3rd
Mobile stress — same root, shifting stress + vowel reduction

Key insight: The same morpheme may have different vowel realizations depending on stress. Unstressed vowels reduce to /ə/ (e.g., démocrat /ɛ/ → democracy /ə/). This sets up the next discussion: stress rules for nouns, adjectives, and verbs.
교재 §7.1 (pp.179) Variable + Mobile stress 소개. (1) Variable: 영어 강세는 polysyllabic 단어의 어느 음절에든 올 수 있음 — article (1st), tomato (2nd), kangaroo (3rd). 게르만 언어 특성. (2) Mobile: 형태론적으로 관련된 단어들이 다른 음절로 강세가 이동 + 동시에 vowel reduction 동반. 핵심 시리즈: democrat/democracy/democratic, origin/original/originality, constitute/constitutional/constitutionality, photograph/photography/photographic, diplomat/diplomacy/diplomatic. 한국 학생들에게: 강세 위치가 단지 음향적 특성이 아니라 모음 질까지 바꿈을 강조. 다음 슬라이드부터 §7.2 명사·형용사 강세 규칙 (penult rule).

§7.1 Introduction — Tendencies, Not Rules

Despite variability and mobility, English stress is not random — but these regularities are tendencies rather than airtight rules. Two factors frequently affect stress placement: a word's grammatical category and its morphological structure. (Yavaš p. 179)

Grammatical category

N vs V — same segmental string, different stress:

a pérmit (N)[pə́ɹmɪt]
to permít (V)[pəɹmɪ́t]

→ §7.2 (N/Adj · Penult Rule), §7.3 (V · Ult Rule)

Morphological structure

Affixes shift / re-place stress:

démocrat[déməkɹ̣æt]
+ -acy → demócracy[dəmákɹ̣əsi]
+ -ic → democrátic[dèməkɹ̣ǽtɪk]

→ §7.5 (Affixes: bearing · neutral · shifting)

This book's approach (Yavaš p. 180): combines syllable-counting (light/heavy, position) with word-class distinctions (N/Adj vs V) — the framework for §7.2–§7.5.
교재 §7.1 ¶4-5 (pp. 179-180): 영어 강세는 무작위 아니지만 'rule'이 아니라 'tendency'. 90%+ regularity 정도. 두 요인: (1) Grammatical category — N/Adj은 Penult Rule (§7.2), V는 Ult Rule (§7.3). 대표 예: permit (N pérmit / V permít). (2) Morphological structure — 접사가 강세 위치를 이동(democrat → demócracy → democrátic). §7.5 접사 분류로 이어짐. ¶5 methodology: 본 책은 syllable counting + word class 둘 다 활용. 일부 책은 syllable만 보거나 word class만 봄. 두 가지를 결합하는 접근. ¶6 (Norman Conquest, Greek/Latin 차용 등 역사적 배경)은 본문에 포함 안 함. 강사 구두 보충 가능. ¶7 (가짜 prefix 단일 morpheme 처리)은 사용자 결정에 따라 생략. 한국 학생들에게: '경향(tendency)' 강조. 다음 슬라이드부터 §7.1의 마지막 prerequisite — stressable syllable과 syllable positions.

§7.1 Introduction — Stressable Syllable & Syllable Positions

Before formulating stress rules, two preliminaries are needed: what counts as a stressable syllable, and how to refer to syllable positions in a word. (Yavaš p. 180)

Stressable syllable (= heavy)
  • Light: non-branching rhyme (short V, no coda) — cannot carry stress if reduced
  • Heavy: branching rhyme — either
      (a) V (≠ [ə]) + coda (simple or complex)
      (b) long V or diphthong (± coda)
  • [ə] is weightless — never carries stress

"Heavy syllables attract stress" (cf. Week 12 §6.7).

Syllable position terminology
  • ult — the last syllable
  • penult — the syllable before the ult
  • antepenult — the syllable before the penult

probability [pɹ̣a.bə.bí.lə.ti]

pɹ̣a.bəbíləti
(pre-antepenult+)antepenultpenultult
교재 §7.1 (p. 180) 강세 규칙 도입 전 두 가지 전제: (1) Stressable syllable — light (non-branching rhyme, short V + no coda) vs heavy (branching: V+coda or long V/diphthong); [ə]는 weightless하여 강세 받을 수 없음. (2) Syllable position 용어 — ult (마지막), penult (끝에서 둘째), antepenult (끝에서 셋째). 교재 예시: probability [pɹ̣a.bə.bí.lə.ti] — anteрenult=bí, penult=lə, ult=ti. 이 두 정의가 §7.2 Penult Rule + §7.3 Ult Rule의 토대.

§7.2 Noun and Adjective Stress — The Penult Rule (1/2): Main Patterns

Penult Rule: In nouns/adjectives, stress falls on the penult if it is stressable (heavy = long V/diphthong, or coda; [ə] is weightless); otherwise, stress falls on the antepenult. (Yavaš pp. 180–183)

Group Pattern Examples
Disyll. NPenult stress (default, 81.7% per Hammond 1999)ágent, bálance, fáther, húsband, spínach, zípper
Disyll. AdjPenult stress (default, 81%)árid, cómmon, próper, hónest, sólid, ámple
3+ syll. (penult heavy)Penult stressed — long V/diphthong or codatomáto, aróma, horízon, agénda, compúter, barracúda
3+ syll. (penult light)Antepenult stressed (penult [ə] weightless)ábdomen, álgebra, ánimal, cómedy, vítamin, aspáragus, thermómeter
Hammond (1999) regularity: Trisyllabic nouns > 90% regular (42% penult, 49.5% antepenult of 2074 words). Trisyllabic adjectives 90%+ (75.3% penult, 15% antepenult of 666). The remaining ~10% are exceptions (next slide).
교재 §7.2 (pp.180–183) Penult Rule. Hammond (1999): 81.7% disyllabic 명사 + 81% disyllabic 형용사 = penult 강세. Trisyllabic+에서도 90%+ regularity (42% penult, 49.5% antepenult). Disyllabic penult 강세 예시: agent, balance, ballad, bottle, cabbage, chicken, cookie, country, father, husband. Disyllabic antepenult 강세 (light penult [ə]): tomato → toMAto, aroma, diploma, horizon, computer, bonanza. Trisyllabic+ antepenult 강세: abdomen, algebra, animal, buffalo, comedy, vitamin. 4+ syll: barracuda (penult heavy), asparagus/apocalypse/thermometer (antepenult). 예외 1 — Place-names: Yokohama, Vienna, Calcutta, Louisiana, Indiana, Montana 등 penult heavy 아닌데도 penult 강세. 추가 [ɑ]/[æ] penult: Morocco, Osaka, Chicago, Havana. 예외 2 — Real exceptions: typhoon, sardine, shampoo, antique, July (ult 강세). Also (mostly French borrowings): clarinet, cavalier, engineer, kangaroo, gasoline, cigarette, magazine, mayonnaise. 한국 학생들에게: '경향'으로 학습 권장 (90%+ regularity but exceptions exist).

§7.3 Verb Stress — The Ult Rule

Ult Rule: In verbs, stress falls on the ult if it is heavy (long V/diphthong or coda); otherwise, on the penult. (Yavaš pp. 183–184)

Group Pattern Examples
Disyll. V (heavy ult)Ult stressed — long V/diphthong or coda (47.6%)achíeve, admít, agrée, annóunce, confíne, digést, inténd, impórt, predíct, replý
Disyll. V (light ult)Penult stressed — ult unstressable (52.3%)bálance, blóssom, bóther, dístance, fúrnish, hárvest, púnish, fígure, súrface, vísit, díffer
Trisyll. VUlt (39.5%) / penult if ult light (40%) / antepenult if both light (18.5%)cf. understánd, devélop, accómpany
ExceptionsPenult stressed despite heavy ultcópy, díagram, wórry, árgue, réscue, stúdy, énvy
Hammond (1999) regularity: The Ult Rule accounts for over 99% of disyllabic verbs (2072 sample); only a small set of exceptions (copy, study, etc.) deviate.
교재 §7.3 (pp.183–184) Verb Ult Rule. (1) Disyllabic V: heavy ult → ult 강세 (achieve, admit, agree, announce, confine, digest, intend, import, predict, reply); light ult → penult 강세 (balance, blossom, bother, distance, furnish, harvest, punish, figure, surface, visit, differ). Hammond 통계: 47.6% (987/2072) heavy ult stressed + 52.3% (1085/2072) light ult → penult stressed = 99%+ regularity. (2) Trisyllabic V: 39.5% (151/378) heavy ult stressed, 40% (157/378) penult stressed (light ult), 18.5% (70/378) antepenult stressed (both light). (3) 예외: 7개 — copy, diagram, worry, argue, rescue, study, envy — heavy ult이지만 penult 강세. 핵심 메시지: 동사는 명사/형용사와 달리 ult 우선 (Yavaš 표현: "verbs focus on the ult").

§7.4 Secondary Stress — (1/2) Framework

Following Ladefoged (2001), the difference between primary and secondary stress is one of pitch, not stress per se. Both syllables have prominence; only the primary carries the tonic accent (major pitch change). (Yavaš pp. 185–186)

Three-feature framework
Type±stress±tonic±full V
Primary+++
Secondary+−+
Unstressed−−± (often [ə])
Numerical system (1–4)
  • 3 pluses → 1 (primary)
  • 2 pluses → 2 (secondary)
  • 1 plus → 3
  • 0 pluses → 4

photography = 4 1 4 3  ·  photographic = 2 4 1 4

교재 §7.4 (pp.185–186) Secondary Stress 개념·framework. Ladefoged (2001) 핵심 통찰: primary vs secondary 차이는 stress가 아니라 pitch — 둘 다 prominence는 있지만 primary만 major pitch change (tonic accent) 동반. 3-feature framework: ±stress, ±tonic accent, ±full V → primary (+,+,+), secondary (+,−,+), unstressed (−,−,±). 숫자 시스템 1–4: 3plus=1 (primary), 2plus=2 (secondary), 1plus=3, 0plus=4. 예: photography [fə.tɑ́.gɹə.fi] = 4 1 4 3; photographic [fò.tə.gɹǽ.fɪk] = 2 4 1 4. 다음 슬라이드에서 secondary stress detection과 패턴.

§7.4 Secondary Stress — (2/2) Detection & Patterns

Detection generalizations (Yavaš p. 186–187): (a) Longest sequence of reduced V is ≤ 2  ·  (b) Full vowel = stressed unless final open / two other prominents / /i, o, u/-[ə] alternation  ·  (c) Secondary typically precedes primary.
Disyllabic words with both stresses

Primary–Secondary (post-tonic):

cráyon, cýclone, fránchìse, émpìre, áspèct, áccènt, éncòre, ássèt, hýgìene

Secondary–Primary (pre-tonic):

bàmbóo, sàrdíne, hòtél, tỳphóon, tàbóo, tràpéze, sùpréme, ràccóon, gòurmét

Long words (4+ syllables)

Secondary falls 2-3 syllables back depending on full vowel:

Àfghanistán, rèconciliátion, èxcommúnicate, expùgnátory, gràtificátion, bìbliógraphy

Special cases
  • Pre-tonic exceptions: martíni, rhinóceros (full V allowed before primary)
  • Primary before secondary, side-by-side: cúcùmber, híeràrchy
Rhythm rule: English avoids stress clash → secondary alternates rhythmically.
교재 §7.4 (pp.186–187) Secondary stress detection + patterns. (1) 3 generalizations: (a) reduced V 연쇄는 최대 2개; (b) full V는 강세 받음 — except final open syllable, word with 2 other prominents, or /i, o, u/-[ə] 교체 환경 (Section 4.9); (c) secondary는 일반적으로 primary 앞에 위치. (2) Disyllabic 양쪽 강세 패턴: (a) Primary-Secondary (post-tonic): crayon, cyclone, franchise, empire, aspect, accent, encore, asset, hygiene; (b) Secondary-Primary (pre-tonic): bamboo, sardine, hotel, typhoon, taboo, trapeze, supreme, raccoon, gourmet. (3) Long words (4+ syll): Afghanistan [æ̀f.gæ.nə.stæ̀n], reconciliation [ɹ̣ɛ̀.kən.sì.li.é.ʃən], excommunicate [ɛ̀k.skə.mjú.nə.kèt], expugnatory [ɛ̀k.spʌ́g.nə.tɔ̀.ɹ̣i], gratification [ɡɹ̣æ̀.tə.fə.ké.ʃən], bibliography [bì.bli.ɑ́.gɹ̣ə.fi]. (4) Special: pre-tonic 예외 — martini, rhinoceros (full V도 pre-primary에 가능); primary-before-secondary 인접 — cucumber, hierarchy. (5) Rhythm rule: stress clash 회피, secondary는 주기적 교대.

§7.5 Affixes — Three Suffix Types

Prefixes do not change word stress. Suffixes fall into three classes by their effect on stress (Yavaš §7.5, p. 187):

§7.5.1
Stress-Bearing (Attracting)

Effect

Suffix attracts the stress (always heavy syllable).

Suffixes

-ade, -aire, -ation, -ee, -eer, -ese, -esque, -ette, -itis, -ific

Example

lemon → lemonáde

§7.5.2
Stress-Neutral

Effect

Base keeps original stress; no change.

Suffixes

All 8 inflectional (-s, -ed, -ing, -er, -est, ...) + derivational (-al, -ful, -ly, -ment, -ness, -ship, -y adj, ...)

Example

háppy → háppiness

§7.5.3
Stress-Shifting (Fixing)

Effect

Stress moves to syllable immediately before the suffix.

Suffixes

-ic, -ity, -ify, -ial, -ian, -ical, -icide, -ious, -ous, -ual, -ometer

Example

périod → periódic

Common element of (a) and (b) [(stress-bearing & stress-shifting)]: both change the location of stress; common element of (b) and (c) [(stress-shifting & stress-neutral)]: both do not carry stress.

접미사의 강세 영향은 영어 어휘 형태론에서 중요한 주제. 교재 §7.5의 세 가지 분류: (a) §7.5.1 Stress-bearing (attracting) — 접미사 자신에 강세, 항상 heavy syllable (-ade, -aire, -ation, -ee, -eer, -ese, -esque, -ette, -itis, -ific). e.g., lémon → lemonáde, Japán → Japanése. (b) §7.5.2 Stress-neutral — 어근 강세 유지. 모든 8개 inflectional (-s, -ed, -ing 등) + 다수 derivational (-al, -er, -ful, -hood, -ish, -ism, -less, -ly, -ment, -ness, -ship, adj-forming -y). (c) §7.5.3 Stress-shifting (fixing) — 접미사 직전 음절로 강세 이동 (-ic, -ity, -ify, -ial, -ian, -ical, -icide, -ious, -ous, -ual, -ometer 등). e.g., périod → periódic. 공통점: (a)+(b) 모두 강세 위치 변경; (b)+(c) 모두 강세를 carry 안 함. (a)+(c)는 root 강세 변경 발생. 학생들에게 -y 주의: 명사 형성 -y는 stress-shifting (homophone → homophony), 형용사 형성 -y는 stress-neutral (silk → silky).

Summary

Key Concepts — Week 13

  • Variable & Mobile Stress: English stress varies across syllables and shifts in morphologically related words (with vowel reduction)
  • Penult Rule (nouns/adj.): stress penult if heavy; otherwise antepenult (Hammond 81.7% disyll. nouns)
  • Ult Rule (verbs): stress ult if heavy; otherwise penult (Hammond 99% of disyll. verbs)
  • N–V Pairs: pérmit (N) vs. permít (V); stress shift signals grammatical category + may trigger vowel reduction
  • Secondary Stress: Ladefoged 3-feature framework (±stress, ±tonic accent, ±full V); rhythmic alternation avoids clash
  • Suffix Types: stress-bearing (-ee, -ese, -ation), stress-neutral (-ness, -ly, -ment), stress-shifting (-ic, -ity, -ify)
