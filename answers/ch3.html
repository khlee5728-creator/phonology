Exercise 1. Consonant Allophony Rules

Complete the following statements and give examples (in phonetic transcription). (Your own examples should differ from the textbook's.) (a) Vowels/diphthongs are longer before ___ stops than before ___ stops. (b) Voiceless (fortis) stops are aspirated when ___ (c) Stops are unreleased when ___ (d) Stops are nasally released when ___ (e) Alveolar stops become dental when ___ (f) Alveolar stops are flapped when ___ (g) /t/ is deleted when ___ (h) /t/ may be replaced by a glottal stop when ___ (i) Velar stops are more front when ___ (j) Velar stops are rounded when ___ (k) Vowels, nasals, and /l/ are longer before ___ fricatives than before ___ fricatives. (l) Stops, fricatives, and nasals are long when ___ (m) Alveolar sonorants become dental when ___ (n) Non-velar nasals become labio-dental when ___ (o) Nasals may be syllabic when ___ (p) Approximants /w,ɹ̣,l,j/ are devoiced when ___ (q) Approximants /l/ and /ɹ̣/ may be syllabic when ___
Answer
#RuleExample(s)
(a)Vowels/diphthongs are longer before voiced stops than before voiceless stops.bad [bæ:d] vs. bat [bæt]; said [sε:d] vs. set [sεt]
(b)Voiceless stops are aspirated when they occur at the beginning of a stressed syllable (syllable-initial before a stressed vowel).pin [pʰɪn], top [tʰɑp], can [kʰæn]
(c)Stops are unreleased when they occur before another stop (in a heterosyllabic stop cluster).stopped [stɑpt̚], bookcase [bʊk̚kes], backdrop [bæk̚dɹ̣ɑp]
(d)Stops are nasally released when they occur before a nasal consonant in the same or following syllable.button [bʌtn̩], hidden [hɪdn̩], sudden [sʌdn̩]
(e)Alveolar stops become dental when they occur before an interdental fricative /θ/ or /ð/.at the [æṯ ðə], width [wɪṯθ], eighth [eṯθ]
(f)Alveolar stops /t/ and /d/ are flapped when they occur between vowels in an unstressed syllable (intervocalic position following a stressed vowel).butter [bʌɾɚ], ladder [læɾɚ], city [sɪɾi], riding [ɹ̣aɪɾɪŋ]
(g)/t/ is deleted when it occurs within a consonant cluster, specifically between two consonants (e.g., in /ntl/, /sts/, /kts/, /fts/ clusters).exactly [ɪɡzækli], softness [sɔfnəs], Christmas [kɹ̣ɪsməs]
(h)/t/ may be replaced by a glottal stop [ʔ] when it occurs before a syllabic nasal or in syllable-final position before an unstressed vowel (in many dialects).button [bʌʔn̩], mountain [maʊʔn̩], kitten [kɪʔn̩]
(i)Velar stops are more front (fronted) when they occur before front vowels or before /j/.keep [k̟ip] vs. cool [kul]; geese [ɡ̟is] vs. goose [ɡus]
(j)Velar stops are rounded when they occur before rounded (back) vowels.cool [k͡ʷul], goose [ɡ͡ʷus], quote [k͡ʷot]
(k)Vowels, nasals, and /l/ are longer before voiced fricatives than before voiceless fricatives.leave [li:v] vs. leaf [lif]; ooze [u:z] vs. use [juz]
(l)Stops, fricatives, and nasals are long (geminate) when they occur across a morpheme boundary where the same segment ends one morpheme and begins the next.bookcase [bʊk:es], unnamed [ʌn:emd], penknife [pεn:aɪf]
(m)Alveolar sonorants (/n,l/) become dental when they occur before an interdental fricative /θ/ or /ð/.in the [ɪn̪ ðə], health [hεl̪θ], tenth [tεn̪θ]
(n)Non-velar nasals (/m,n/) become labio-dental [ɱ] when they occur before a labiodental fricative /f/ or /v/.comfort [kʌɱfɚt], infant [ɪɱfənt], envelope [εɱvəlop]
(o)Nasals may be syllabic when they occur after a stop in a final unstressed syllable (typically after /t,d,n/ in words like -ten, -den, -n).button [bʌtn̩], hidden [hɪdn̩], seven [sεvn̩]
(p)Approximants /w,ɹ̣,l,j/ are devoiced when they occur after a voiceless consonant (in the same onset cluster).play [pl̥e], try [tɹ̣̊aɪ], twin [tw̥ɪn], cue [kj̥u]
(q)Approximants /l/ and /ɹ̣/ may be syllabic when they occur after a consonant in a final unstressed syllable.bottle [bɑtl̩], little [lɪtl̩], table [tebl̩], center [sεntɚ]

Exercise 4. Lateral and Nasal Plosion

Circle the items that qualify for lateral and/or nasal plosion. State the generalization. puddle, bottle, goggle, apple, head lice, deep lake, red light, pickle, widen, listen, loosen, channel, muddle, Jordan, ignite, climb, shouldn't, needle, nickel
Answer

Lateral plosion occurs when a stop (/t,d,p,b,k,ɡ/) is immediately followed by /l/ in the same syllable or across syllables where the lateral forms the syllable nucleus. The stop is not released orally but the closure is maintained while the tongue sides lower for /l/.

Nasal plosion occurs when a stop is immediately followed by a nasal (/n,m/) in a syllabic position, where the nasal becomes syllabic.

bottleYesLateral plosion/t/ + syllabic /l/ → [bɑtl̩]
goggleYesLateral plosion/ɡ/ + syllabic /l/ → [ɡɑɡl̩]
appleYesLateral plosion/p/ + syllabic /l/ → [æpl̩]

deep lakeNo—Word boundary; /l/ is onset

pickleYesLateral plosion/k/ + syllabic /l/ → [pɪkl̩]

listenYesNasal plosion/t/ (often deleted) + syllabic /n/ → [lɪsn̩]

Generalization: Lateral/nasal plosion occurs when a stop is immediately followed by a syllabic sonorant (/l/ or /n/) in final unstressed syllables. The closure of the stop is maintained; release happens through the sonorant (laterally for /l/, nasally for /n/).

Exercise 8. Extended Transcription Passage

Transcribe the passage about "the spread of English" (continued) from Trudgill & Hannah (2002): "It was also during the 1800s that the development of Southern Hemisphere varieties of English began..."
Answer

This is an extended open transcription exercise. Key consonant processes to apply:

  • Flapping: development [dɪvεlәpmәnt], varieties [vәɹ̣aɪɪɾiz], Australia [ↄstɹ̣eɪliə], later [leɾɚ], settled [sεtl̩d→sεɾl̩d]
  • Nasal assimilation: colonization — /n/ before /k/: [kɑlәnaɪzeʃәn], final /n/ before bilabials
  • Stop deletion/unreleasing: colonized final /d/ before /f/: unreleased; Scotland /t/ before /l/
  • Key content words: varieties [vәɹ̣aɪɪɾiz], colonization [kɑlәnaɪzeʃən], similarities [sɪmɪlærɪɾiz], Australasia [ↄstɹ̣eleʒiə], hemisphere [hεmɪsfɪɹ̣]
