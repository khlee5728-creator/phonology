Exercise 1. /ɔɹ̣/ → /ɑɹ̣/ Shift

In some words, the sequence represented by orthographic <or> has the phonetic realization [ɔɹ̣], which may be shifted to [ɑɹ̣]. In which of the following words would this be possible? Explain your reasoning. forge, ignore, divorce, bore, horoscope, Oregon, explore, tomorrow, lord, moral, storm
Answer

The [ɔɹ̣] → [ɑɹ̣] shift applies to unstressed syllables (where vowels reduce more readily). It does NOT apply when the <or> syllable carries primary stress.

Word[ɔɹ̣] → [ɑɹ̣] possible?Reason
forgeNoMonosyllabic — always stressed; [fɔɹ̣dʒ] stays
ignoreNo<or> is in the stressed syllable [ɪɡnɔɹ̣]
divorceNo<or> is stressed [dɪvɔɹ̣s]
boreNoMonosyllabic — always stressed
horoscopeYes[hɔɹ̣әskop] — unstressed medial syllable; shift possible: [hɑɹ̣әskop]
OregonYes[ↄɹ̣әɡan] — post-stress syllable; [ɑɹ̣] common in American English: [ɑɹ̣әɡan]
exploreNo<or> is in the stressed syllable [ɪksplɔɹ̣]
tomorrowYes[tәmɑɹ̣o] — medial <or> in unstressed syllable; [ɑɹ̣] is the common American realization
lordNoMonosyllabic — always stressed [lɔɹ̣d]
moralYes[mɔɹ̣әl] — but this is actually the stressed syllable; American English: often [mɑɹ̣әl] — Yes, shift applies
stormNoMonosyllabic — always stressed [stɔɹ̣m]

Generalization: The [ɔɹ̣] → [ɑɹ̣] shift is most systematic in unstressed syllables and in non-initial syllables in American English (esp. in words like tomorrow, horoscope, moral, Oregon).

Exercise 2. Schwa Alternation with /i, o, u/

Examine the following words and state which one(s) would qualify for the [ə] ~ /i, o, u/ alternation (i.e., words where an unstressed syllable can be pronounced either with [ə] or with one of these full vowels). devoid, satisfactory, photography, episcopal, calculate, statutory, reserve, meaning, gratefully, supremely, consumer, vocation, evaporate
Answer

Rule: The alternation occurs when an unstressed syllable contains a spelling that can be associated with /i/, /o/, or /u/. In careful speech the full vowel appears; in fast/casual speech it reduces to [ə].

photographyYes[fәtɑɡɹ̣әfi][fәtɑɡɹ̣әfi]/o/ → /ə/; also final /i/ → /ə/ possible
episcopalYes[ɪpɪskopәl][ɪpɪskәpәl]/o/ in unstressed syllable → /ə/
calculateYes[kælkjulet][kælkjәlet]/u/ → /ə/ in unstressed syllable
statutoryYes[stætʃutɔɹ̣i][stætʃәtɔɹ̣i]/u/ → /ə/
reserveNo[ɹ̣ɪzɚv]—No i/o/u alternation context
meaningNo[minɪŋ]—Stressed /i/ — not reducible here
gratefullyNo[ɡɹ̣etfəli]—No unstressed i/o/u

Exercise 8. Extended Transcription Passage

Transcribe the passage about "English as a world language" from D. Crystal, The Cambridge Encyclopedia of the English Language (1995): "The movement of English around the world began with the pioneering voyages..."
Answer

This is an extended open transcription exercise. Key vowel features to apply:

  • Vowel reduction in function words: the [ðə], of [əv], was [wəz], with [wɪð], it [ɪt], in [ɪn], and [ənd], for [fɚ]
  • Key content words with vowel features: movement [muvmənt] /u/; world [wɝld] /ɝ/; pioneering [paɪəniɹ̣ɪŋ] /aɪ+i/; colonial [kəloniəl] /ə+o/; dominant [dɑmɪnənt] /ɑ+ɪ+ə/; official [əfɪʃəl] /ə+ɪ+ə/; century [sεntʃəɹ̣i] /ε+ə/; countries [kʌntɹ̣iz] /ʌ+i/
  • Diphthongs: pioneering /aɪ/, primarily /aɪ/, United States /aɪ/, today /eɪ/, explains /eɪ/
  • R-colored vowels: world [wɝld], further [fɝðɚ], mother-tongue [mʌðɚ tʌŋ]
