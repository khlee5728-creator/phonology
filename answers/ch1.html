Exercise 13. Multiple Choice

(a) Tensing the vocal cords makes them vibrate faster/slower, so that the pitch increases/decreases. (b) In the production of stops/fricatives/glides/affricates, the air is blocked from going out through the nose and the mouth. (c) In the production of stops/liquids/fricatives/nasals, the constriction of the vocal tract is such that a noisy airstream is formed. (d) In the production of palato-alveolar sounds, the tip/front/blade/back of the tongue goes to the forward part of the hard palate/soft palate/uvula. (e) In the production of labio-dental/bilabial/labio-velar/velar sounds, the two lips approach one another, and the back of the tongue is raised toward the soft palate.
Answer

(a) Tensing the vocal cords makes them vibrate faster, so that the pitch increases.

(b) Stops — airflow is completely blocked at both the oral and nasal passages (velum is raised, blocking nasal cavity; articulators create full oral closure).

(c) Fricatives — a narrow constriction creates turbulent (noisy) airflow.

(d) The blade of the tongue goes to the forward part of the hard palate.

(e) Labio-velar — both lips approach one another AND the back of the tongue is raised toward the soft palate (e.g., /w/).

Exercise 15. Extended Transcription Passage

Transcribe the passage about "the spread of English" from Trudgill & Hannah, International English (2002): "The English language developed out of Germanic dialects that were brought to Britain, during the course of the 5th and 6th centuries..."
Answer

This is an extended open transcription exercise. Key phonological features to apply:

  • Vowel reductions: Function words (the, of, that, were, by, and) use schwa /ə/ in connected speech.
  • Flapping: /t/ and /d/ between vowels in unstressed syllables → [ɾ] (e.g., Britain [bɹ̣ɪɾәn], medieval).
  • Consonant clusters: Apply regular English cluster rules; note <ths> in "5th" = [fɪfθ].
  • Final consonant devoicing/unreleasing: In pre-consonant position (e.g., "brought to" → [bɹ̣ɔt̚ tʊ]).
  • Sample key words: English [ɪŋɡlɪʃ], language [læŋɡwɪdʒ], Germanic [dʒɚmænɪk], dialects [daɪәlεkts], centuries [sεntʃәɹ̣iz], replaced [ɹ̣ɪplest], original [ɚɹ̣ɪdʒәnәl], Celtic [kεltɪk], survived [sɚvaɪvd], Scandinavian [skændәnevɪәn].
